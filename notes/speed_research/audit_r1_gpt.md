## 1. Audit of Claude's seven ideas

### C1 — game clock, then fixed-fps acceleration: conditional; reject the broad rewrite first

**What holds and why:** explicit mixed clocks in the baseline make timing equivalence a real concern. A fixed-step candidate is more promising than blindly raising time_scale because preserving simulation resolution matters. This is evidence for an experiment, not evidence that physics, input edges, rendering and timers remain equivalent, nor that 1.8–2.5x is achievable. 112 rendered FPS with two games does not measure accelerated physics throughput.

**Break it:** a wall-time gameplay deadline can become looser in simulated seconds, causing a false PASS. A sample that occurs every rendered frame can observe different physics states; a plant may become weaker. Host-driven inputs can arrive at different simulated times. Headless/low-resolution output can invalidate visual checks. Conversely, wholesale changes to gameplay clocks can change the original faults before discovery: the benchmark then compares different games and can miss or misattribute holes. A grep reaching zero ticks is neither necessary nor sufficient: reporting/focus clocks should remain wall time, and time can enter through timers/helpers too.

**Change:** classify clock uses by purpose and dependency first; keep host health clocks in real seconds and preserve gameplay assertion meanings. Test one unchanged nonvisual domain with fixed stepping before any production rewrite. Leave rendered/aim/reach/video domains at baseline settings until separately qualified. No gameplay clock replacement merely to make acceleration pass; any real design bug needs its own disclosed fix and check. Gate on measured speed and clean/plant differential evidence. Build 45–90+ minutes, I think; abandon if the prerequisite becomes a general timing refactor.

### C2 — compile once per unchanged state: holds in mechanism; magnitude unproved

**Reason:** run_test.sh really launches an import before each run; identical code/asset inputs within a proof batch need not require repeated identical imports. But the comment '~40 s' is not a fresh measurement; Godot's cache may make repeats much cheaper.

**Break it:** omitted asset, deletion, generated/untracked file, engine version, project/import settings or damaged cache makes the key unsound. A watcher catching script errors does **not** prove safety: a stale asset can load without errors and make an unobserved check falsely pass. Import failure must not create a successful cache entry.

**Change:** begin with one explicit successful import for an immutable proof batch, fingerprint all inputs and relevant engine/import state, force import on uncertainty. Cache validity must include file removals and cache absence, not just tracked edits. Do not add a general persistent cache until repeat-import timings justify it. 10–20 minutes build, I think. Savings 0–15 minutes across A/C/D/F; count actual skipped process lifetimes, not 40 seconds times a guessed launch count.

### C3 — early plant termination and batching: accept early termination conditionally; defer batching

**Reason:** baseline proof asks for specific failures and already batches nonmasking plants; work after a complete, valid detection may be unnecessary. There is no new gain from simply saying 'group plants'.

**Break it:** an early text match can occur before the relevant observation window completes, or identify a different failure. A generic H-rule receipt can be caused by another active plant. Merely knowing the active plant set and timestamp does not distinguish causes within the same set. Killing at the first match can hide a subsequent TEST/health failure and prevent final buffered evidence being written. Switching plant flags does not undo instantiated geometry, accumulated marks, helpers, watches or contacts; contamination produces false PROVED or false alarms.

**Change:** retain baseline groups/process separation initially. Early exit requires every expected check's exact identity, scenario, activation/reach evidence, completed required observation interval and persisted receipts; no earlier health/TEST condition. Label it 'plant detection complete; remaining scenario not executed', never clean PASS. Compare with a full proof control, verify process gone, and require matching clean controls at the final revision. Ambiguous grouped causality requires an isolated plant run, whose cost counts. 20–40 minutes build, I think. D may save 4–10 minutes from early exit alone; C2 is separate shared overhead. Do not promise 27 -> 12 without measuring failure positions.

### C4 — first/last suites, selective fixes, continue after stall: partially merge, remove automatic continuation

**Reason:** GHAT selection and ordinary-FAIL collection already support targeted iteration. Retaining a final full suite protects coverage. An initial suite can find independent failures; it is useful discovery, but its 20 minutes must earn its place.

**Break it:** a call graph misses indirect state, reset/save/input and shared vehicle dependencies; late regressions force another expensive suite. A STALL/TEST is an off-plan run: automatically relaunching later scenarios before diagnosis violates the stop-and-look rule and can repeat infrastructure failures. Fresh-process successes also cannot erase a same-process ordering failure.

**Change:** default to one final suite, with deliberate broad walk/design discovery before it; authorize an initial suite only when its expected discovery benefit justifies the measured cost. Unknown/shared dependencies select broadly. After a stall: stop, capture, diagnose, then explicitly run remaining independent discovery work; preserve the failed scenario and original order failure in the report. Separate discovery receipts from final acceptance. A mapping/receipt implementation costs 20–35 minutes, I think. A can save 20 if retaining an initial suite, or ~39 gross if not; extra targeted work reduces that saving. C gains overlap short cases.

### C5 — note-and-repair walk: accept bounded discovery; reject guaranteed exhaustive repair-through

**Reason:** one early blocked action need not prevent inspecting a genuinely independent later branch. The walk has distinct note, hide, cap, spring and marks work. Collecting several independent issues can reduce repeated diagnosis, even with resume already supplied.

**Break it:** invoking real SaveGame/dev functions does not make the substituted state reachable or causally equivalent. Repairing an encounter phase can bypass the very state transition containing a hole; faults may manifest much later than the proposed 10-second warning. A script error or unexplained movement needs diagnosis, not a repaired continuation. BROKE on many steps may describe one cause, not many holes.

**Change:** stop actions immediately, preserve the first-failure state/picture, and continue only by a justified independent branch with listed setup and BLOCKED upstream dependencies. Carry diagnostic provenance through all later steps, without a time expiry. Use resume for normal repairs. Final acceptance starts fresh and has no substituted repair state beyond the baseline's already declared setup. Savings **beyond supplied resume**: B 5–15 and F 2–5 minutes, I think; the original 35–45 cannot be credited again. Build 25–45 minutes, I think.

### C6 — record the final clean play: accept; retain a fresh final take

**Reason:** an equivalent recorded walk can serve both final input proof and review video, eliminating a duplicate clean play. Baseline Movie Maker settings already differ from normal play, so equivalence needs checking rather than assuming identical coverage.

**Break it:** lowering resolution can weaken visual evidence. Changing all clocks is not a guarantee against recording-only bugs. A stitched resume demonstrates suffixes and restores; it does not demonstrate one successful continuous causal path. The referee reports h1 stitching works technically, not that a stitched take alone satisfies clean-walk acceptance.

**Change:** qualify a short clip, keep required visual quality/sound, then record the final fresh walk and watch it. Keep stitched parts as clearly marked diagnostics or optional review material, with a fresh run still required for acceptance unless the referee explicitly changes that contract. Quit and verify exit before encoding/review. E saving 4–10 minutes, I think; eliminated duplicate clean playback is B and counted once. Build 10–25 minutes, I think.

### C7 — trace, pictures, no idle game: accept narrow instrumentation

**Reason:** a state/event trace and failure angle can answer a diagnostic question without replay; idle process minutes unquestionably count. This supports the mechanism, not the 10–20-minute forecast.

**Break it:** 2 Hz misses short transitions; three observer shots can delay stopping or show the wrong view. Trace values share the game's instrumentation mistakes and cannot establish visual truth. Logging/image overhead can erase savings, especially in a fast run. A live world can move away from the failure while the camera is being changed, giving wrong blame.

**Change:** capture the failure state/time immediately, include event-time transitions and player views, use existing observer shots where appropriate, bound capture time and shut down after evidence is secured. Never wait for three shots before stopping unsafe/off-plan actions. Keep periodic human watching every few seconds; automated limits supplement it. Add only fields needed to answer declared questions. Build 20–40 minutes, I think. B/F savings 3–10 minutes, overlapping C5 and short cases; measure overhead and replay avoidance.
